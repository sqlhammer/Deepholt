# Story & Setting

Tone target: **warm and adventurous throughout** — Core Keeper's register. The mountain's
history is a catastrophe, but the game treats it with wonder and curiosity rather than grief
or dread. Ruins are *marvels to reclaim*, not graves to mourn. Nothing down there hates you.

---

## 1. The situation

The molepeople have lived in this mountain for a very long time. They used to live in **all**
of it — a warren running from the summit shelf down past the mountain's base, with foundries,
terraces, aqueducts, and a lift network that ran the whole depth.

Generations ago, that ended. What's left of the people lives on **Level −1**, a thin shelf
just under the summit: a few dozen families, subsistence fungus, tools they can no longer
make replacements for, and stories about the levels below that have decayed into folklore.

You are a moleperson of that upper shelf, with a shovel, a claim, and an unreasonable idea.

## 2. The disaster, in three acts (revealed in reverse)

The player uncovers this backwards, one stratum at a time. Nobody ever explains it in a
cutscene.

**Act I — They dug too greedily.**
The Works expanded faster than it shored. Spans got wider, columns got thinner, and the load
paths were never recalculated. Eventually a hall came down, and it took the hall below it,
and that took the one below *that*.

> *This is the player's own core verb, done badly. Every support column you place is an
> argument with your ancestors. The game teaches structural integrity as a mechanic, then
> shows you the consequence of ignoring it as a place.*

**Act II — The collapse opened the water.**
The cascade tore through the seal on the mountain's aquifer. The lower warrens flooded — not
in a day, but faster than the bulkheads could be closed by people who no longer understood
the sluice system that had been maintained for centuries.

**Act III — Some of them didn't get out.**
The evacuation went up. Most made it. Below the water line, a community was cut off — and,
because they were molepeople, they did not die. They sealed, they adapted, they farmed the
dark, and they have been down there for generations building something of their own.

They are not monsters. They are not ghosts. They are **strangers with the same grandparents**,
and they have technology the upper shelf forgot.

## 3. The ending: reconnection, not conquest

The climax of the game is not a boss. It is the moment your pumps, your shoring, and your
restored lift shaft break through to a lit, inhabited hall that has been sealed since before
your grandmother was born.

This is the single most important design consequence in this document:

> **The entire tech tree — drainage, sealing, deep shoring, lifts, power — is the story.**
> It is not a means to the story. Reaching the bottom *is* engineering, and engineering is
> what the plot is about. No other system needs to carry narrative weight.

It also feeds the settlement layer: the deep community is the source of the most skilled
molepeople you can recruit, and of the techniques that upgrade your own warren. Reconnection
pays out in *people*, which is exactly what the deferred worker system needs.

## 4. Cast & factions

| Who | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **The Upper Shelf** | Level −1 | Your people. Small, cautious, out of ideas. Source of early quests, the first traders, and mild scepticism about your project. |
| **Wanderers & hermits** | Surface | The ruined world above has a handful of survivors — scavengers, a travelling trader, an eccentric who lives in a broken tower. Small vendor lists, POI anchors, the first recruitable outsiders. |
| **The Deep Warren** | Levels −6/−7 | Cut off for generations. Their own culture, their own tools, their own opinion of you. The endgame reveal and the best recruits in the game. |
| **The mountain's fauna** | All levels | Not evil. Worms, crickets, blind things, something large in the water. Hazards with ecology, not an army. |

## 5. Storytelling method

- **No cutscenes.** Environmental storytelling and found objects.
- **Ruins tell it.** A workshop with tools set down mid-task. A terrace of homes with the
  doors barred *from the outside* — going up. A pump station with a hand-scratched duty
  roster that stops on a particular day.
- **Recovered documents are blueprints.** Story text and tech unlocks are the same pickups,
  so reading is never optional-flavour-text-you-skip.
- **Warm framing.** Even the disaster sites read as *impressive* — "look what they built" —
  before they read as *sad*. Keep the lighting warm and the music curious.

## 6. Naming (working)

Strata names are placeholders pending an art/naming pass: Rootshelf, The Greyseam, The Old
Terraces, The Works, The Drowned Reaches, The Crush.

The game is titled **Deepholt** ([D-027](./design-decisions.md)) — a *holt* is an animal's den,
so the name reads literally as "the deep den". **Tunnelkin** is held as a secondary favourite.
